Can you explain the chain rule again? I'm having trouble applying it to $f(x) = \sin(x^2)$.

Of course. The chain rule is used when you have a composite function—a function inside another function.

The rule is: $\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$

For your function, $f(x) = \sin(x^2)$:

  • The "outer" function is $\sin(u)$. Its derivative is $\cos(u)$.
  • The "inner" function is $u = x^2$. Its derivative is $2x$.

So, putting it together:
$f'(x) = \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x = \mathbf{2x \cos(x^2)}$
